Dead Tree Introduction

Forest managers consider not only the yield of wood products, but also how management decisions affect other forest values. Dead trees have important ecological roles such as food source, nesting substrate, and microclimate modification. Silvicultural decisions (e.g., establishment density) influence the availability of dead trees. To assist forest managers, TIPSY includes dead tree information.

TIPSY includes three options to explore dead tree dynamics: Mortality, Snags, and Coarse Woody Debris (CWD).

The Mortality Table is an interpolation of mortality tables generated from TASS (i.e., similar to how live tree yield tables are generated).

The Snag Table provides a stand table by dbh size classes of the number of standing dead trees expected at an age step. The expected number is derived from an interactive logistic model.

The CWD (Coarse Woody Debris) Table lets you can view the data as:

  • the amount of deadwood (i.e., snags) that becomes CWD (CWD Periodic Recruitment Table), or

  • the amount of CWD present at an age step given the decay Soundwood losses due to physical and chemical processes caused by wood-rotting fungi. of CWD over time (Cumulative Volume of CWD [less decay]). The Cumulative Volume Table can also report all CWD, or deadwood that became CWD during a specified period.

Also note that TIPSY disregards the fact that breakage of a tree or bole can occur over a long time period. The volumes reported are based on the complete bole being present when a tree dies and falls.

Snag dynamics (i.e., fall and fragmentation rates) in TIPSY are limited by TIPSY's configuration (e.g., database size, interpolation process with sparse matrices) and also by the lack of data on dead tree dynamics in British Columbia. TIPSY's parent model, TASS, provides greater flexibility for modelling dead trees but is also limited by available information.

For more information about Dead Tree Dynamics visit the Ministry's Dead Tree Website.
