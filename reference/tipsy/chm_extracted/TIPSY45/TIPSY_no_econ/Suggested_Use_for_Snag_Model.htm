Snags: Model Uses

Growth and yield models project expected conditions. Forest stand and site conditions are variable. Mortality is highly variable. As such, don't expect a growth and yield model like TIPSY to precisely project an individual stand. TIPSY is best used to evaluate silvicultural options in managed stands by comparing treatments within the program. Including a standing dead tree model within TIPSY lets us compare an environmental value (i.e., number of standing dead trees) with wood production values.

The Snag Model's default parameters are based on limited data. The fall rate of dead trees is highly variable depending on factors such as the source of mortality and local wind conditions. You should investigate how changes in the model parameters (i.e., assumptions related to the dead tree fall down) influence the treatments you are considering.

A good example of using a growth and yield model to look at environmental impacts of stand tending is found in Greenough and Kurz (1996). This study used TASS, and a standing dead tree model in TASS, to investigate the effects of a variety of treatments on timber production and numerous environmental indicators. The environmental indicators were simple index models related to different species and stand conditions (e.g., large down wood). The study demonstrates how, with limited information, we can use a model to increase our understanding. It also appropriately identifies limitations of the results found in the modelling exercise.

Linkages to Other Models

Information in the Mortality and Snag Tables can be used in conjunction with other models. For example, you might want to know how the density of standing dead trees relates to the habitat requirements of different woodpecker species.  Models such as DecAID or Snag Recruitment Simulator module SRS2 translate desired population levels of several woodpecker species into minimum standing dead tree requirements. These requirements can be compared with TIPSY's tables.

Use caution when extrapolating from models developed for specific regions.
