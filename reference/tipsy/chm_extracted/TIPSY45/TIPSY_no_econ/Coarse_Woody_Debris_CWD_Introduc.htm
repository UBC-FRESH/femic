CWD: Introduction

Coarse woody debris (CWD) plays many valuable ecological roles including habitat for invertebrates, microsite for plant establishment, and structure in stream and river systems. Knowledge of the dynamics of CWD over the life of a forest stand can provide valuable insight into decisions on CWD maintenance.

You can access the CWD Output Table from the Tables Menu or from the toolbar using this icon

TIPSY can generate two different types of CWD tables: CWD Periodic Recruitment and Cumulative Volume of CWD (less decay). The CWD Periodic Recruitment table displays the volume or number of snags that became CWD between the age steps, or rows, in the table. The values in this table come from the snag table.The Cumulative Volume of CWD (less decay) option reports either:

  • the volume of CWD (less decay) accumulated since age 0; or

  • the volume of CWD (less decay) that accumulated during a specified time period.

When you select the Specify recruitment age option, each row in your table reports the volume of CWD (less decay) that accumulated between From years and To years prior to the reported age step in the table. The steps that appear in your table were selected earlier in the Table Specifications dialog box.

TIPSY assumes that there was no CWD present on the forest floor at stand establishment. TIPSY's snags and coarse woody debris calculations do not affect biomass and carbon output.

Unfortunately, TIPSY ignores the thinnings from precommercial thinning in its coarse woody debris model, but we may include them in future releases. Commercial thinning will reduce the number of trees 'eligible' to become CWD.

We use the term CWD for large down woody materials (i.e., logs) only. In some literature, the term CWD also includes snags (standing dead). CWD is generally considered to have some minimum size characteristics (e.g, >10 cm diameter). In our tables we do not exclude small woody debris in our total figures, but we do provide a separate breakdown by dbh size classes.

Please note that CWD in TIPSY is derived only from tree boles (i.e., no large branches) and that snags fall only once as a whole tree. No allowance is made for fragmentation. Allowing only whole trees to fall has the net effect of delaying the input (recruitment) of CWD and the start period for CWD decay.
