Site Index: About

Site index is the species-specific measure of Site Productivity The inherent capability of the land to grow trees, governed by the interaction between climate, geography, soils, and genetics. used in TIPSY.  It is the key variable governing height growth in TIPSY.   As such, site index has a major impact on yield predictions, so it is important to provide the best site index estimate available.

Definition

Site index is a measure (index) of a site's inherent potential productivity for a particular tree species.   This inherent potential is an interaction of local geography, soils, climate, and genetics.

In BC, site index is defined as Top Height, at breast-height age 50, of a stand that has been free from (significant) height-growth-altering factors since establishment.  This is best characterized by dominant and codominant trees in single-species, even-age stands that have been (relatively) free from competing vegetation, repression, genetic improvement, fertilization, and top-damage.  These criteria help ensure past height growth has not been confounded by these other factors, which alter the estimate of inherent site potential.

Site index equations are used to estimate site index from field measurements of age and height.   Visit the SITETOOLS website for more information on site index estimation tools for BC.

Implications

Obviously, many operational stands do not meet the criteria above.  Stands failing to meet these criteria produce inaccurate site index estimates.  E.g., site index estimates from old-growth inventories commonly underestimate the productivity of post-harvest managed stands.  Fortunately, several methods (e.g., SIBEC) have been developed for indirectly estimating site index in the absence of appropriate trees.

Always select the best site index estimate available to help ensure the best yield estimates from TIPSY.
