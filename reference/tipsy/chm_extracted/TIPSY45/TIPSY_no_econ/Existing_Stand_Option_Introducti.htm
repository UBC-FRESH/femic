What it Does

The purpose of the Existing Stand Option is to estimate an initial Density value for an existing (established) stand using its current density, site index, and age (or height).   To accomplish this, TIPSY essentially "ungrows" the stand.

The process of moving backwards to establishment is complicated.  As TIPSY "ungrows" the stand, it must also "undo" any treatment responses or other factors that affected stand growth in the past. Consequently, users must first define all these factors within TIPSY's normal settings, before using the Existing Stand Option.   Once the initial Density is estimated,  TIPSY reintroduces the same growth factors as it projects the growth of the stand from establishment.

Use Notes

Top Height vs. Age and Site

If you know top height from field measurements, enter it directly in the Existing Stand Option instead of having TIPSY calculate it from age and site index. You can also "unlock" site index and have TIPSY calculate it from age and top height. This will, of course, override the site index specified initially. You can't unlock site index for a stand that was genetically improved or fertilized.  Also, don't use top height and age measurements from genetically improved, fertilized, or repressed stands to determine site index.

Known Density vs. Top Height

Since the combination of density and height must fall within a range of data, one variable will determine the valid range of the other and vice versa. TIPSY automatically adjusts the range or value of one variable to conform to the other.

Density Limits

Values in the following examples may not match those in the current version of TIPSY, but the concepts still hold true.

A pine plantation, for example, has an available range of Known Density (before thinning) of 261 to 4027 trees/ha at the default BH Age and Top Ht. (5.4m). If you specify a Known Density of 3000 trees/ha and then enter a Top Height of 20 m in the Age/Height dialog box, TIPSY will have to lower your original density from 3000 to 2190 trees/ha to conform to the upper limit of density at a height of 20 m. Always check the density and height values before the Existing Stand Option calculates the planting density (e.g. 4444 trees/ha).

Always establish the planting density before using the Existing Stand Option to calculate the pre-commercial thinning density, because the former determines the available range of residual densities. If you plant pine at 2500 trees on site 20, your range of residual densities at a top height of 20 m will be 312 to 1 847 trees/ha. Plant 2000 trees/ha and the limits become 312 and 1376 trees/ha.
