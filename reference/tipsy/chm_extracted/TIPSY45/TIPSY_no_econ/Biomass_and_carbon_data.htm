Biomass models

To incorporate the biomass and carbon prediction capabilities into TIPSY, a new yield table database was generated with TASS.  The growth of approximately 12 million individual trees was simulated to generate a total of 1659 yield tables counting all the species, treatments and different site indices.  It took about 2500 hours to generate these runs.  

The above-ground biomass for each live and dead tree was calculated using the existing DBH- and height-based individual tree biomass equations developed by Ung et al. (2008).  In this project, we use Ung's model for a total of 14 species as shown in Table 1.

 The equation form is:

(1)  yi = βik D βik H βik + εi

where yi is the dry biomass of component i for either: stem wood, stem bark, foliage or branches (kg), D is diameter at breast height (DBH, cm), H  is total tree height (m), βik are the parameter estimates ( i is as above, k = 0, 1 or 2), and εi is the error term for the component i. Total stem dry biomass was calculated as the sum of the stem wood and stem bark, while the total above-ground biomass was calculated as the sum of the total stem, foliage and branches biomass.

The total below-ground root biomass for each tree was calculated as a function of the total above-ground biomass using the equations developed by Li et al. (2003) for all softwoods and hardwoods.  The equations forms are:

(2)  RBs= 0.222ABs

(3)  RBh = 1.576ABh0.615

where RB and AB are root and above-ground biomass (kg) respectively, s is the softwood species group, and h is the hardwood species group respectively.  The parameter estimates for these models by species and for all softwoods and all hardwoods are included in Table 2.

Carbon Model

To convert biomass to carbon stocks for each individual tree component a conversion factor for temperate zones of 0.5 g C/g (Matthews, 1993; Lamlom and Savidge, 2003) was used as follows:

(4) Carbon stock = biomass * 0.5

TIPSY Assumptions and Reporting

When a stand is fertilized, TIPSY uses a height 'bump' to achieve the specified fertilizer response.  All variables, including biomass, are bumped to the same point on the yield curve.

Dead tree values are calculated from the mortality table in TIPSY.  Snags and coarse woody debris are not considered.

The carbon content of minor vegetation, soil, dead organic matter, and litter are not considered.

Live and dead tree biomass values for wood, bark, foliar, branch, and roots are interpolated from values the TIPSY database, which were generated with biomass models in TASS.   Above-ground and total biomass values are summed by TIPSY.  Carbon values are all calculated by TIPSY by multiplying biomass X 0.5.

OAF effects

Mortality is partially affected when operational adjustments factors (OAFs) are used.  For instance, when an OAF2 or a custom OAF is selected, the OAF reduces all biomass including the dead biomass, then the dead biomass and carbon will increase by the same amount lost in live biomass and carbon columns.  In other words, the dead trees that are moved to the mortality table are also reported as dead biomass and carbon stock.  The exception is when the custom OAF is set to 'remove' the volume as in a salvage operation.  This 'removal' OAF removes only wood and bark; other components are moved into dead biomass.

In the topic Impact of Adjustment Factors there is a table that lists which TIPSY output variables are affected by OAFs, including Armillaria, and custom OAFs.  When using custom OAFs, pay attention to the option to either 'Move trees to the mortality table' or 'Remove trees from the stand completely'.  These options are described here.

TIPSY vs. CBM Model

Differences between TIPSY/TASS and the Carbon Budget Model of the Canadian Forest Service include:

- merchantable trees vs whole stand including non-merchantable and small trees

- individual tree dbh & ht used to calculate biomass vs. net merch volume

- age-dependant mortality vs. consistent mortality rate

- deadwood doesn't decay vs decays

- snag fall down rates are probably different
