Genetic Worth Settings

The Genetic Worth dialogue box is accessed via a button on the Species Specification dialogue box.  Genetic worth can only be applied when Planted has been selected as the regeneration method in Stand Specifications.

The Genetic Worth dialogue box has two settings:   Genetic Gain (%) and Selection Age.   Default values are species-specific, but are only intended for exploratory and educational purposes.  Defaults may not be suitable for operational applications, such as timber supply analyses.  Genetic Gain values should be obtained from the Seed Planting And Registry System (SPAR) instead.

More About

Genetic worth is a value used to describe the quality of genetically improved seed.  It represents the percentage volume gain expected at an index age near harvest.

The Ministry's Tree Improvement Program has been developing genetically improved seed over the past 30 years by selective breeding of seed collected from superior wild trees.  The Tree Improvement Program maintains the Seed Planting And Registry System (SPAR), an online database of seedlot information that includes genetic worth and selection age.   Seedlot identification numbers are used to access this information.

The calculation of genetic worth assumes that the percent height gains recorded at the selection age will decline with time because there is a risk of not selecting the best trees. Conversely, the likelihood of identifying superior trees increases as the selection age approaches the index age.

Options and Limitations

Genetic worth cannot be applied to natural stands (natural or clumped).

Multiple species can be used to specify improved and unimproved versions of the same planted species.   Be cautious applying genetic worth to multiple species with dissimilar growth habits, i.e., the ability to compete for light, moisture, and nutrients, which would normally produce changes in species composition over time.   Species composition is static in TIPSY.

Volume gains are referenced to a specific age (60 or 80 years), site index and treatment regime. Gains will be higher or lower for other conditions. See How Stands Respond for more information.

Species substitution (BatchTIPSY) does not extend to genetic worth.  If no genetic gain data exists for a species, TIPSY defaults to 0, even if it uses the yield database of another species.

with Commercial thinning

In order to achieve the additional volume growth generated by genetic gain and/or fertilization, TASS accelerates height growth. This method of implementing genetic gain or fertilizer is complicated by the sharp decline in standing volume after commercial thinning.

Minor anomalies can occur when commercial thinning is applied to stands with complex prescriptions. Examples are stands with multiple fertilizer applications or stands that were regenerated with improved planting stock and have one or more fertilizer applications. Under these conditions, the reported percentage gain in periodic growth of fertilized stands might not coincide with the percentage you have specified, or even the percentage identified in the table header.

Incremental gains can vary by up to three percent when fertilizer is applied after the stand is commercially thinned. However, volume growth in this period is low due to the age of the stand and the reduced number of trees. As a result, these differences are largely inconsequential relative to total volume production.
