Selecting the Right Model

Use TIPSY Table Interpolation Program for Stand Yields at the stand level to evaluate silvicultural options in managed stands of pure species.   Be aware that the multiple species feature was designed as an stand aggregation tool for forest-level planning; it does not reflect species dynamics at the stand level.

Customized TASS Tree and Stand Simulator runs can extend the range of silvicultural practices and treatment levels available in TIPSY. See Beyond TIPSY.

Use VDYP Variable Density Yield Projection, a growth and yield model for natural stands in BC and TIPSY together at the forest level for timber supply analyses of natural refers to trees or a stand that regenerated naturally from seed after disturbance, e.g., fire or loggingand managed stands, respectively, adjusting TIPSY yields with OAFs to reflect operational conditions. Although TIPSY does address managed stands of natural origin, including the untreated controls, do not use the two models to compare the performance of managed and natural forests at the stand level because each represents a different level of yield as described in TIPSY vs VDYP.

Forest level planning

Use VDYP for natural stands and TIPSY for managed stands. Since VDYP is based on randomly located inventory plots, its yields incorporate the full range of stand conditions encountered in naturally regenerated forests. Consequently, VDYP predicts the average yield of untreated forests of natural origin.

TIPSY, in contrast, is based on growth trends observed for up to 70 years in fully stocked research plots growing in a relatively pest free environment. The yields are expected to be very close to the potential of a specific site, species and management regime. These yields are generally not attainable under operational conditions. However, they are the only source of data for managed stands subjected to various silvicultural treatments. We can predict the yield of our best managed stands with reasonable confidence.

The reduction necessary to approximate the yield of the average managed stand is not known. Our best estimate suggests that the productivity of the average managed stand will be about 15% below the unadjusted yields generated by TIPSY. For forest planning purposes, we recommend that yields be reduced by Operational Adjustment Factors (OAFs) of 15% and 5% for OAF 1 and OAF 2 respectively, unless there is strong evidence that local conditions warrant a different value. Reduce these OAFs if using other adjustment factors such as DWB.  See Operational Yields for more detail on using OAFs.

It’s tempting to compare the curves for natural stands generated by TIPSY and VDYP. A casual visual comparison shows that VDYP produces approximately 70% of TIPSY’s yield for roughly similar conditions. This implies that the productivity of unmanaged stands of natural origin is about 30% below the site’s potential. More detailed comparisons are not warranted. Natural yield curves from TIPSY may be used for forest-level planning to address stands that regenerate from seed at acceptable densities. However, OAFs should be applied in most cases.

Stand level planning

VDYP is generally not useful at the stand level since it does not support silvicultural treatments.

Use TIPSY for managed stands. TIPSY provides yield curves for evaluating treatments such as planting densities and pre-commercial thinning. Yield curves for untreated, naturally regenerated stands are also included in TIPSY to provide a baseline for treated stands.  TIPSY’s yield statistics help forest managers evaluate the merits of silvicultural treatments in terms of yield and harvest age.
