Variable Retention: Assumptions & Limitations

TIPSY's VR adjustment factors (VRAFs) reflect several VR modelling assumptions that affect yield projections and their interpretation.

TIPSY's VRAFs reflect:

  • only the harvested and regenerated portions of the stand, based on the standard VR assumption that trees are retained for other values and will not be harvested.   VRAFs (<1.0) reflect regenerated (i.e. understorey) yields relative to a clearcut (no overstorey retained).

  • a reduction in the initial number of trees In TIPSY, it refers to the number of living trees in an even-aged stand composed of a single cohort. This includes all trees that regenerate naturally over a short period of less than 5 years, or all trees planted in a particular year. It does not include those which seed in later. All trees start as dominants and later progress to lower crown classes and die in response to intertree crowding. Dead trees are not counted. The number of trees can be reduced by thinning. present in the stand, which will differ from the initial density value specified by the user.

  • single-species stands with the same retained and regenerated species and site indices.   For multiple species stands, VRAF is pro-rated based on species composition.   The leading species is used to calculate edge length.

  • model coefficients for only four species: Hw, Sw, Fdc, Pl.  TIPSY automatically makes species substitutions for others.

  • systematically planted regeneration with random ingress.

  • the TASS II assumption that the sun is always directly overhead and that shorter trees do not survive within the vertical canopy shadow of taller trees.  In effect, TIPSY VRAFs remove the vertical crown shadow of the retained trees from production. For dispersed retention in particular, you might consider this effect too extreme and adjust inputs accordingly.

  • defaults which can be overridden by the users.   Default edge-length is calculated based on 40% crown cover.   Other defaults include a residual top height at entry of 30m, an aggregated retention of 100% (no dispersed retention), and an average retained group size of 500m2.

TIPSY's VRAFs do not reflect:

  • pest (e.g., mistletoe) interactions that sometimes accompany variable retention practices.

  • modifications such as topping or pruning for wind firmness.  However, the effect would likely be quite small, since retained tree crown growth accounts for less than five percent of the total yield adjustment due to variable retention.

  • change in overstory crown cover during the regeneration delay Natural stands: years from disturbance (fire, logging) until the first successful seed year. Plantations: years from disturbance (fire, logging) until planting period (if any), but this is normally negligible.

  • retention already included in existing allowances for riparian zones, wildlife tree patches, etc.   E.g., timber supply analyses.

  • values outside TIPSY's minimums and maximums for top height, CC%, retained height and edge length.   When these limits are exceeded, TIPSY adjusts values to within acceptable species ranges, and notes this in the Stand Description.

Cost considerations for FAN$IER

The Appraisal Manual used to derive FAN$IER's default tree-to-truck costs also contains 'loose' estimates for partial-cut harvesting that add $2.71/m3 for skidders and $3.62/m3 for highlead or grapple yarders.    User may wish to apply these additives to FAN$IER's tree-to-trunk defaults in the absence of better local information.  But remember, these are harvesting costs for the regenerated portion of the stand.
