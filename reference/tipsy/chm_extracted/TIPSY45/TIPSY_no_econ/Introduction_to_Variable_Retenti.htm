Variable Retention: Introduction

Variable retention (VR) is a silvicultural system that mimics nature by retaining some forest structure after harvesting. Standing trees are left in a dispersed and/or aggregated patterns to meet objectives such as retaining old growth structure, habitat protection and visual quality. Structural features remain (snags, large woody debris, live trees).

The main objectives of VR are to maintain the natural range of stand and forest structure and their associated functions. Thus, retention patterns are determined before deciding which areas to harvest.  VR can be implemented with a range of harvesting systems and can be combined with other silviculture systems such as shelterwood or selection.

TIPSY yields reflect only the harvested and regenerated portions of the stand, based on the standard VR assumption that trees are retained for other values and will not be harvested.   It is considered a cost of doing business and gaining certification.   If the retained overstorey will eventually be harvested, the silvicultural label should reflect other closely related strategies such as shelterwood or partial cutting.

TIPSY Variable Retention Settings accommodate two variable retention patterns:

Dispersed retention - retains individual trees scattered throughout a cutblock;

Aggregated (group) retention - retains trees in clumps or clusters.
