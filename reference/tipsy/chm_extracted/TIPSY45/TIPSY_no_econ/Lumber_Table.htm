About Lumber Tables

The Lumber Table is accessed through the Tables Menu or the Tool Bar icon above.  This table reports the conversion of trees (12.5+ cm) into lumber, which is reported as  #2 & Better volumes plus associated chip volume.

For conifer-deciduous mixes, lumber is only reported for the first species in the Species Specifications list.  

The last column, Lumber Recovery Factor the number of board feet of lumber recovered from each cubic metre of standing volume at a particular age. (bd ft/m3) (LRF, bf/m3), is the total lumber volume (bf/ha) divided by the standing merch volume [12.5+cm], which approximates total log volume [12.5+cm].

Small discrepancies between log and lumber tables are unavoidable since different bucking, scaling and waste assumptions are used to calculate volume. For example, the populations of logs going into the log tables is different from the population of logs used to generate lumber tables because the log bucking algorithm maximizes log value in one case and lumber value in the other. Consequently, log volumes would differ between the two tables, if both were reported.

Custom Table Options

Three (3) types of lumber summaries, plus Mill Residue By-products of lumber manufacturing including: chips, sawdust, shavings, trims, and bark.s, are available through Custom Tables:

Lumber (all grades)

Lumber volumes (bf/ha) for four (4) lumber dimensions (2x4, 2x6, 2x8, and 2x10) within each of five (5) lumber grades (SS, #1, #2, #3 and #4).   These lumber values form  the basis for the two other lumber types below.

Lumber, #2 & Better

#2 & Better lumber volumes (bf/ha) represent a roll-up (summation) Lumber values above for grades SS, #1, and #2 only; grades #3 and #4 are not included.  #2 & Better is also the only type reported in Lumber Tables.

Mill Residues

Mill Residue By-products of lumber manufacturing including: chips, sawdust, shavings, trims, and bark.s are reported in both the Lumber table and Custom Tables:

  • Custom Tables provide a full component breakdown, i.e., Chips, Sawdust, Shavings, Trims, Bark, in oven dry tonnes per hectare (odt/ha).

  • The Lumber table only reports the Chip component.

Multiple Species

For multiple species stands, TIPSY reports lumber volumes for all species combined. TIPSY processes each species in the stand as a pure species, then prorates the lumber volumes according to the percent composition specified in Species Specifications.  However, lumber values are sent to FAN$IER by species to enable separate pricing.

Armillaria OAF Impact

The apparent impact of Armillaria root rot on Lumber Recovery is not real. It is an artifact of OAFs altering the distribution of trees across diameter classes without affecting the log and lumber tables as one would expect. Consequently, log and lumber volumes will be slightly high, and economic analyses will understate losses accordingly. In effect, we’ve added a model (i.e., Armillaria) to TIPSY that can't be applied consistently to every dependent variable and table.

Aspen

TIPSY does not provide aspen lumber recovery information.  Aspen logs are normally manufactured into OSB (oriented strand board), LVL (laminated veneer lumber), LSL (laminated strand lumber), and plywood, as well as into pallet and container lumber.

Red Alder

Red alder lumber dimensions are reported as 1x4, 1x6, 1x8, and 1x10, instead of the usual 2x dimensions.  This reflects the marketing standards for hardwood lumber, which also uses different grading rules based on usable content, rather than undesirable qualities such as knots, wane and splits. Each grade of hardwood lumber is determined from the size and number of clear wood cuttings which a board can produce.  The TASS sawmill simulator used to produce TIPSY lumber tables is not able to grade hardwoods. The default grade used for alder was "U" grade which is a lower quality softwood lumber grade. It does not represent the present day value nor approximate hardwood grading rules. Therefore, the TIPSY lumber statistics for red alder should not be considered an accurate reflection of hardwood grades and market value.   See Red Alder in TIPSY for more information.
