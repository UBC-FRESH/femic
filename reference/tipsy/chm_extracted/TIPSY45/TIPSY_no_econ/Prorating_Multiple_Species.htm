When stands containing multiple species are defined in Species Specification or BatchTIPSY, the resulting yields are pro-rated.   This feature was originally added at the request of timber supply analysts so they could easily aggregate several stand types into fewer analysis units.

How it Works

TIPSY adopted a simple pro-rating method for calculating multiple species yields similar to the one used by VDYP.   Multiple-species yield tables are constructed by pro-rating independent single-species yield tables based on Percent Composition values.   In other words, area-weighted averaging of single species yields.  The following simplified diagram illustrates this area-weighting.   It shows how two species, with Percent Composition values of 25%  and 75%, share one specified initial density (1600 stems/ha).   However, the two species do not actually share the same physical hectare.  Therefore, they do not interact biologically and no species dynamics or succession occurs.

Like VDYP, this method also incorporates site index conversion to predict the Site index (SI) a measure of site productivity based on the top height [meters] of a stand at breast-heightage 50. It does not apply to stands in which the height growth of top height trees has been affected by treatment (fertilization, tree improvement) or stand density (repression). of one species from that of another.  Although, predicted site index values can be over-written when better information exists.

TIPSY can pro-rate a maximum of 5 species. Coast and interior species can be combined even though it may not be practical.  Deciduous (aspen and alder) can be combined with conifers (note implications below).  TIPSY can only pro-rate yield tables that are based on age.

When pre-commercially thinning a multiple species stand, be aware that thinning takes place at different ages for each species. This is because TIPSY thins at a fixed height (4m or 6m), which translates into different ages for each species. Note, pro-rated yield tables will not display pre- and post-PCT data in this case.

Limitations and Implications

As noted above, this simple aggregation of single-species yields assumes species composition is fixed over time.  This does not reflect real-world species dynamics or succession, which may preclude its use in some stand-level silviculture applications.  Within broad-scale forest-level applications, it may still be useful especially when species have comparable life spans (e.g., most conifers in TIPSY).  However, extra caution is warranted when combining dissimilar species such as alder and aspen with longer-lived conifer species.  Note, TIPSY applies pre-defined Age Decline Custom OAFs to both alder and aspen by default.

The limitations of this method are illustrated below with reference to the total volume of stands of lodgepole pine (site index = 20), and white spruce (converted site index = 19.59) planted with 750 or 1500 trees per hectare. This example was created with a previous version of TIPSY.

Pure stands of pine and spruce planted with 750 trees yield 395 and 425 m³ respectively at age 80. The "mixed" stand (PlSw750) with 375 trees of each species produces 410 m³ which is exactly midway between the volumes of the pure stands. There is no opportunity for the mixed stand to exhibit synergism in TIPSY. It’s not surprising that the mixed stand with 750 trees of each species produces more volume than a pure stand of either species planted with 750 trees (486 vs. 395 and 425 m³ at 80 years). This simply shows that dense stands (e.g. 1500 trees/ha) produce more volume than widely spaced stands (e.g. 750 trees/ha). The difference is even more pronounced at young ages.

It is important that silviculturists understand TIPSY does not simulate the growth of mixed species stands biologically. Consequently, we recommend that the multiple species feature not be used in the preparation of silvicultural prescriptions, or at least only with a full understanding of the limitations and risks involved.

Continuing research may show that TIPSY's multiple species feature can be used at the stand level for:

  • species with similar growth habits that compete equally for aerial and subterraneous resources. At present, this is a very tenuous assumption since changes in species composition and other important variables are likely to occur as the stand develops.

  • "coarse-grained" mixtures where trees of a particular species tend to be aggregated in very small clumps throughout the stand - as opposed to a "fine-grained" composition where the species are intimately mixed.

Yield Table Calculations

Here is an explanation of how TIPSY builds a multiple species yield table internally. This example was created with a previous version of TIPSY.

Assume you have a stand of 5000 trees regenerated naturally, composed of 60% lodgepole pine and 40% white spruce. Height and age measurements of pine indicate a site index of 13 m. Corresponding data are not available for spruce.

1. TIPSY generates a yield table internally for the reference species, pine.

2. Next, it calculates the corresponding site index for spruce (i.e. 11.99 m), and then produces a second table internally.

3. Lastly, TIPSY takes 60 % of the yield of pine, and adds 40 % of the yield of spruce to generate the yield table that appears on your screen.
