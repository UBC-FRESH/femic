Mean annual increment (MAI)

MAI is the ratio of volume over TIPSY Age; it is reported in Yield Tables and Custom Tables.   Each type of volume and Merchantability Limit has a unique MAI.  

Culmination age determination

MAI culmination age is the age of maximum MAI.  This defines a regime's physical rotation, i.e., the rotation length that maximizes volume production in perpetuity.   Culmination ages sent to FAN$IER are calculated from annual volume data, rather than the age steps defined in Table Specifications.   

Trivial fluctuations in the TIPSY database affect MAI and the determination of culmination age.   In the extreme example below (created by an earlier version of TIPSY), MAI (column 5) increases yearly to a maximum of 16.38 at age 56 (apparent if MAI were displayed more precisely), declines ever so slightly until age 62, increases to age 67, and then goes into a very slow decline. MAI is essentially flat from about age 54 to 76. However, random perturbations due to the death of individual trees in TASS simulations cause the MAI to fluctuate when we engage in such "superfluous precision". In reality, we should plot the MAI/age relationship and subjectively identify the age that coincides with the beginning of the "plateau" (e.g. 55 years).

  • Linear interpolation. TIPSY employs linear interpolation to obtain information that falls between entries in its database. This technique forces the culmination of MAI to coincide with an entry in the database. That is, the point of maximum MAI will increase in irregular age steps that correspond to height steps of 2 m (interior) or 3 m (coast) in the database. Higher resolution in the database and curvilinear interpolation techniques may be considered in the future to address this minor shortcoming.

  • Age or height steps in yield tables. In earlier versions of TIPSY, the age or height sequence specified for yield tables could affect the MAI sequence because MAI was only calculated at each step in the table, and the maximum could be missed if it occurred between 2 steps. Now, MAI is calculated annually as illustrated in the example below, so the maximum MAI is no longer affected by the age or height sequence in the yield tables.
