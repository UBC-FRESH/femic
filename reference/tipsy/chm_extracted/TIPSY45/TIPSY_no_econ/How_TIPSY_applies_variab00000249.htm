Variable Retention: Programmer's Perspective

There is variable retention data in TIPSY and BatchTIPSY for four species: coastal Douglas-fir, western hemlock (both coastal and interior), lodgepole pine and white spruce. An internal calculation is performed using a pre-defined formula with coefficients based on the species. This results in a reduction factor which is then applied to the stand.

Application of variable retention in TIPSY is done as follows. The reduction factor for any given row is split into three sub-factors which, when multiplied together, will yield the original factor.

________________________________________________________________________

Factor

Definition

Calculation

________________________________________________________________________

fo

the original reduction factor

 

f1

plantable area

1 - crown cover percentage of the retained overstorey

f2

trees that die due to overtopping by the retained portion of the stand

(fo + (f1- fo)/2) / f1

f3

trees that grow more poorly than the rest of the stand due to shading from the retained portion of the stand

fo / (fo + (f1 - fo)/2)

________________________________________________________________________

As an example, if fo was .5 and the crown cover percentage of the retained understorey was 30%, the sub-factors would be .7, .857 and .833 respectively.

These sub-factors are applied in a three-step process. First, the factor f1 is uniformly applied to the stand at all ages. This reduction is applied irrespective of the species in the stand. The reduction will affect the volume/age curve but not its shape, as curves are affected by the same percentage over all ages. It will not affect prime tree statistics or lumber recovery factors.

Second, to model the loss of trees due to overtopping, the tables are reduced by multiplying all statistics by f2 except prime tree statistics, lumber recovery factors and mortality. The resultant loss in stem counts and volume are reflected in an increase to the mortality figures.

Last, all volume data is multiplied by f3 to simulate an overall volume loss without a loss in the actual number of trees (poor growing conditions). This will affect total volumes (0.0+ and 7.5+), all stock values (see Stock Tables), and all log and lumber. Stand mortality is unaffected by this step.

The cumulative reduction factor (fo) applied at age 100 is displayed on the Stand Description.

Note that application of variable retention will reduce the initial number of trees present in the stand, and that this number will differ from the initial density value you set.

In the case of a mixed-species stand, variable retention will be calculated on a species-by-species basis. For each species, the program will treat that species as the overstorey and understorey species for the purposes of calculation. If you want to calculate edge length using the formula, the leading species will be used for those calculations.

We have developed VR coefficients for only four species in TIPSY. If you use variable retention for any other species, the program automatically makes substitutions. See Table of VR species substitutions.
