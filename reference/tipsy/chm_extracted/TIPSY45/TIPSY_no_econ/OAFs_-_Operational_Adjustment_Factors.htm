TASS Rational for OAFs

OAFs (Operational Adjustment Factors) are used to adjust yields for a wide range of abiotic and biotic factors that cause TIPSY predictions to differ from operational yields.

Types of OAFs

TIPSY currently supports two basic OAFs, OAF1 and OAF2, plus Custom OAFs.  OAFs are simple multipliers with a neutral (no adjustment) value of 1.0.

OAF1

A constant multiplier applied uniformly at all ages.  The ministry default, OAF1=0.85, is commonly applied in forest-level and stand-level analyses.  OAF1 is used to reduce yields for various abiotic and biotic factors not accounted for by TIPSY.  Small, unmapped non-productive areas (NP, e.g., rock and wetland) are normally the dominant abiotic factor, but it can also include weather-related stocking loss (e.g., wind, snow, ice).  Biotic factors include competition-related stocking gaps on otherwise productive ground, e.g., growing space occupied by non-commercial species or brush, and endemic losses, e.g., forest health impacts, etc.   Impacts of Adjustment Factors explains how OAF1 affects TIPSY's various outputs.

OAF2

A progressive multiplier; its value and impact increase with age.  The ministry default, OAF2=0.95, is commonly applied in forest-level and stand-level analyses to account for merchantable volume losses due to decay, waste, and breakage (DWB).  However, OAF2 can also be used for endemic biotic factors (e.g., forest health impacts) with yield effects that increase over time.

An OAF2 value is indexed to age 100, such that its specified value (e.g., 0.95) equals its yield impact at age 100.  The impact increases 1% per year reaching the specified value at age 100, and continues increasing by 1% per year thereafter. E.g., for an OAF2 of 0.90, yields are multiplied by 0.999 at age 1, 0.95 at age 50, 0.90 at age 100 (the index year), and 0.80 at age 200, etc.  Impacts of Adjustment Factors explains how OAF2 affects TIPSY's various outputs.

NOTE:  Annualized OAF2 values are based on germination age for both planted and natural stands.  Consequently, it is not affected by regeneration delay, age of planted seedlings, or the length of the germination period for natural stands.   However, OAF2 is not applied until Merch Vol (12.5+) exceeds zero.

Combining OAFs

Combining OAF1 and OAF2 is common practice.  The standard ministry defaults are normally applied together.  

The standard ministry defaults (0.85 & 0.95) are intended to represent "average" operational stand conditions.  Adjustments for extra-ordinary conditions generally involve professional judgement supported by field data.  Users developing TIPSY yields for use in ministry applications are urged to consult application-specific policy before deviating from the defaults.  Click here for the history behind the defaults.

The OAF Impact variable within Custom Table reports combined OAF values by year.  It combines OAF1, OAF2, and Custom OAFs.   However, OAF Impact is not reported until OAF2 takes effect, i.e., Merch Vol (12.5+) exceeds zero.

NOTE: Individual OAFs may be greater than 1.00, but the combined (net) effect cannot increase yield.   Therefore, TIPSY caps the annual net effect at 1.00.  A message appears when this occurs.

See Combining OAFs for an example.

Key OAF concepts

  • With the notable exception of non-productive (NP) ground, OAFs are simplistic surrogates for highly complex biological effects.  OAFs are intended to be temporary placeholders until research-based models for these individual biological effects become available.  A few such models have been incorporated in TASS (e.g., spruce weevil, etc), but not in TIPSY, as yet.  

  • In effect, an OAF can be visualized as removing all the trees from the specified percentage of each hectare, similar to a virtual patch cut.  I.e., the total stand area remains the same, and the residual stand structure and residual stocking density are unaffected.  Consequently,

  • OAFs mainly affect per hectare values, and have little affect on mean tree values,

  • OAFs affect per-hectare yields and mean annual increment (MAI), but OAF1 has no effect on MAI culmination age, a common definition of rotation age.

TASS Rational for OAFs

Differences between predicted and operational yields are to be expected for two basic, interrelated reasons:

  1. Much of the field data used to calibrate TASS comes from permanent plots that were intentionally located (biased) to monitor tree growth relatively free from confounding effects, such as stocking gaps and forest health impacts.  Unavoidably, some of these effects are still present, but they are commonly at lower levels than those encountered in operational settings.   Hence, the need for OAFs when making operational yield predictions.  See OAF History for more information.

  2. TASS calibration actively seeks to minimize these confounding effects by focusing on the best (maximum) growth trends observed within field data.  This is referred to as simulating "potential yield".  In contrast, other types of growth and yield models generate "average yields", which may be more confounded by factors such as stocking gaps and forest health impacts.   Potential yield provides a more consistent baseline for modelling basic (unconfounded) tree growth.  This biological baseline becomes the foundation for modelling growth modifying effects (i.e., spatial distribution, silviculture treatments, forest health, etc).
