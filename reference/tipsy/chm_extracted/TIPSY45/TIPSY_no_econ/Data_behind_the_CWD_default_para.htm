CWD: Data behind defaults

The defaults used for the annual decay Soundwood losses due to physical and chemical processes caused by wood-rotting fungi. rate constants in the CWD model were derived from discussions with various British Columbia and Pacific Northwest USA researchers. The defaults are broad generalizations with some substantiation from existing literature. The white spruce default is used for red alder and trembling aspen, and should be adjusted since alder and aspen decay very quickly.

Decay rates are based on volume loss and are considered independent of piece size although the literature suggests that such decay rates are correlated with piece size.

In British Columbia we have little data to confirm these decay rates. For coastal Douglas-fir on Vancouver Island, a decay rate of 0.02 (2%) per year was found appropriate for residual CWD left in a stand harvested in the 1890's (Stone et al. 1998).

The decay rate constants used in the model are as follows:

Other species in TIPSY will use the decay rate constants of the species used as its substitution database. For example, mountain hemlock uses the decay rate for western hemlock.

Note that a decay rate of 0.02 per year infers a slower rate of volume loss than does a rate of 0.04 per year.
