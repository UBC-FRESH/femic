What is Repression?

Repression is defined as a reduction in height growth due to excessive establishment density. It affects all trees in the stand, including dominants.

Height repression has been documented in fire-origin stands of lodgepole pine, which have been observed to regenerate at up to 800,000 stems per ha.  Although suspected in other species, such as coastal hemlock, it is only well documented in lodgepole pine.   In extremely dense pine stands, height growth repression begins before trees reach 1m in height.  The onset of repression occurs later at lower densities.

Repression in TIPSY

TIPSY applies its repression model only to natural stands (regen method = Natural, but not Clumped) lodgepole pine with initial Densities greater than 13,825 trees/ha (average inter-tree spacing less than 0.8505 m).   At 250,000 trees/ha, the maximum initial Density supported by TIPSY, repression begins when the stand reaches 0.74 m in height.   At lower densities, repression begins later.

The repression effect in TIPSY is avoided only if the stand is pre-commercially thinned (PCT) before the onset of repression, at the density-dependent "critical height".   After this point, PCT will not avoid repression.

When PCT is selected, the Residual Density dialog box offers the option to thin before or after the "critical height".   For stands spaced later, or left unthinned, the actual (repressed) growth falls short of the potential (unrepressed) growth.

A special repression summary appears in the Site Index dialog box when repression is activated. It reports the base (unrepressed) and actual (repressed) top heights projected to breast-height age 50, as well as the loss of top height due to repression. If you make changes to density that effect the repressed top height, this dialogue box will reappear each time just to let you know that top height has been revised.

NOTE:  Repression is not applied in TIPSY to multiple species stand. Also note that it is possible to enter an unreasonably high top height at 50 years (e.g. 30m) for a repressed stand. TIPSY will then display an unreasonably high site index (e.g. 35m) which exceeds the normal upper limit of 30m for lodgepole pine.

Repression Modelling

Critical Height

Stem analysis and plot reconstruction data show that response of dominant trees with crowns extending above the general level of the canopy and receiving full light from above and partly from the side; taller than the average trees in the stand with crowns well developed height growth to thinning is very difficult to predict.  There are few long-term permanent plots in thinned pine stands available for model development.   Based on our available data (Mitchell and Goudie 1980) we therefore assume that repression is avoided only if stands are thinned before the onset of repression.

We used the Barnes Creek data to develop a model to predict the critical top height (THcrit.) at which repression starts.

THcrit = 1.3777*exp(-0.000002468*Density)

where

Density = number of trees established/ha.

For example, if establishment density is 250 000/ha, repression begins at a top height of 0.74m.  In TIPSY, you can choose to thin before or after THcrit. The stand proceeds on the reduced height growth path if the height at time of thinning is greater than Thcrit. and does not respond in height growth (but diameter growth is greater). The stand grows unrepressed if enough trees are removed before it reaches the critical height.    The fact that TIPSY actually applies PCT in yield tables at a height of 4m is not a problem since stand statistics themselves are relatively insensitive to the age of thinning.  For repression, TIPSY does not display output data prior to its thinning height of 4m.

Site Index

Carlson and Johnstone (1983) established the only known experiment in B.C. that specifically addresses repression. They planted from 2500 to 160 000 stems/ha at Barnes Creek. Fourteen year remeasurement data were used in conjunction with growth intercept relationships developed by Nigh (1995c) to derive an adjustment for dominant trees with crowns extending above the general level of the canopy and receiving full light from above and partly from the side; taller than the average trees in the stand with crowns well developed height at breast height achieved at age 50.

Each plot was evaluated using the equivalent of the largest 100 trees/ha by diameter (top height). For the smallest plots, this equated to the height of the single largest tree. The following segmented regression model was fit to the data:

The data indicated that on this site, TH50 increases for initial spacings less than the predicted join-point spacing of 0.8505m, (13 825 stems/ha) and remained constant for wider spacings. The join-point coefficient was solved simultaneously with the regression coefficients and the function is continuous and differentiable at that point.

We have no controlled data from other locations. We used an interim measure to translate the results of the Barnes Creek data to other sites. We converted the results from absolute TH50 to relative (TH50r) by dividing the data by the plateau (21.954m). This results in:

In TIPSY, if the average inter-tree spacing is below 0.8505m and no thinning is scheduled, then the potential top height at age 50 (site index) will increase by the predicted proportion. This is because, operationally, we can measure or calculate the actual top height, but not usually the potential top height of old stands or recently-regenerated stands. For example, if a stand with a projected or actual top height at age 50 of 20m regenerates to 250 000 stems per ha, the potential top height (site index) increases to 20/0.8550 = 23.39 (an increase of 3.3m). This is the site index that would be used for stands that are thinned before repression occurs. This example was created using TIPSY 3.0.

Thinning the stand early avoids repression. The model's inherent assumption that the absolute reduction is lower on poorer sites is supported by available literature on repression (Udell and Dempster 1987, Goudie 1996). We assume that the results from these extremely dense plantations also apply to natural stands of unknown spatial distribution.

The next approach assumes that you know the breast-height age, top height and initial density of a repressed stand, but not the site index. Inventory foresters who collect data from existing stands will find it particularly relevant.

Choose the Actual Top Height (repressed) option, then enter breast-height age and actual top height in the dialog box. Notice that TIPSY calculates the site index and base top height.

The second method holds top height constant as you change initial density in a series of runs. TIPSY adjusts site index to conform to each initial density. It uses breast-height age, actual top height and initial density to project the actual top height to breast-height age 50. TIPSY grows the repressed stand along the site curve which conforms with actual top height - not potential top height. It also calculates the base, or unrepressed, top height at breast-height age 50 (site index) for your information.
