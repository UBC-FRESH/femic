Beyond TIPSY

What other options are available when TIPSY does not support the stand conditions and/or treatments of interest?

The species, silvicultural practice or treatment level may be outside of the database, or the variable of interest may not be among the standard summary statistics.  If so, contact User Support.  Suggested options will likely fall into one of these categories:

Substitution: It is often possible to select a closely related regime which gives reasonable results. For example:

  • If the species is not available, another species in TIPSY’s database may provide comparable yields.  See Species and Species Substitution.

  • When an earlier thinning is prescribed, use the thinning option in TIPSY since time of treatment does not affect the yield appreciably if completed before the canopy closes.

Try TASS III:  A new Windows version of TASS, called TASS III, is now available for a limited number of species.  TASS III is designed to simulate multi-cohort stands.

Customized TASS II run: TIPSY’s database consists of a small subset of the tables that can be generated by TASS II. There are other treatments such as pruning, and an impressive selection of output variables and reports.

Contact User Support to initiate a special request, keeping in mind that a straightforward run (e.g. thin at a later age) will require about an hour of staff time to prepare the input, execute the job and send the results to you. Simulation of ingress, or a particular spatial distribution involving a specified level of clumping, may take longer. We also undertake more comprehensive projects for clients if adequate time and resources are scheduled in advance.

Users expect TASS and TIPSY to give the same results for identical runs. TASS, however, is not configured to conform with the assumptions embedded in TIPSY. TASS cannot operate the same as TIPSY when the height growth of dominant trees is affected by treatment (i.e. fertilization and the use of genetically improved planting stock). If you request these treatments in TASS, you’ll notice that the two systems report different yields, particularly at low establishment densities. In this situation, TASS assumes that high establishment density followed by high mortality leaves a taller/more vigorous cohort of crop trees than a stand established at a relatively low density. This is a result of "natural selection". TIPSY does not allow establishment density to influence the height and vigour of the crop trees - assuming the comparison is limited to the same number of the largest trees (e.g. 100 largest, 250 largest, etc.).

Visit the TASS website for more information.  Our Growth and Yield Modelling Website also includes a list of other regional models.
