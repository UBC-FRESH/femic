Questions and Answers about Site Index

What is site index (SI) and what is it used for?

SI is a measure of a site's productive potential for a particular tree species. SI in British Columbia represents potential (site tree) height at 50 years breast height age. SI enables standardized comparisons of potential site productivity, across a broad range of existing stand conditions.  As such, SI is used as a silvicultural tool to prescribe treatments and analyze investments. SI is a main driver (input) within many growth and yield models, which predict future forest growth and timber yields.

Why is SI so widely used in forestry?

  • Stand height is closely related to stand volume.  Therefore, it is a good proxy for productivity in terms of timber volume.

  • Compared to other measures (diameter, volume, basal area, etc.), stand height is relatively independent of stocking density, except at the extremes for some species.

What factors determine SI?

SI is species specific.  It reflects inherent physical site factors, such as climate (solar input, precipitation, temperature, growing season, etc.) and soil (moisture, nutrients, geology, etc.). SI is generally assumed to be independent of current stand conditions and management.

What about inventory SI?

Forest inventories in BC, and other Canadian provinces, commonly derive Site Index from stand-level height and age estimates obtained via aerial-photo interpretation, because intensive ground sampling is prohibitively expensive at large scales.   Consequently, the lack of site tree selection means forest inventories often underestimate Site Index.  This lead to the development of indirect Site Index estimation methods, such as SIBEC.

How are SI estimation functions derived?

Modern SI functions are derived from historical height growth data gathered through extensive stem analysis (sectioning) of carefully selected site trees.   Each SI function is calibrated across a specific age range determined by the original data.   Exercise caution when making extrapolations outside this range.
