More about GI

Growth Intercept (GI) models provide an alternate (optional), generally more accurate, method for estimating site index from breast height age (1-50yrs) and height In SiteTools, height generally refers to site height..   However, the formulation of GI models is not as flexible as conventional Site Index models.  I.e., GI models can only be used to calculate site index.  They cannot be inverted to calculate breast height age or height In SiteTools, height generally refers to site height., like conventional Site Index models.  GI models are also not available for all species.

Implementation in SiteTools

Growth Intercept (GI) models are implemented in SiteTools (Sindex) in conjunction with conventional Site Index models.   I.e., calculations utilize Growth Intercept (GI) models from 1-50 yrs (breast height age) and conventional Site Index models over 50 yrs.

SiteTools further restricts the application of GI models to:

  • species with GI models in SiteTools (Sindex)1
  • one Calculation method:  Site Index - by age and height
  • calculations involving breast height age (not total age).

1 GI models can only be paired with Site Index models for the same species.   I.e.,  GI models developed for Douglas-fir can only be paired with conventional Site Index models for Douglas-fir, not other species.   However, a same-species GI-SI pair can be assigned to codes for other species in the Species Assignment Table.

More about GI

The growth intercept technique was first developed in the 1930's.   For species with distinct annual whorls, site index estimates in young stands can be obtained quickly through direct observation of past height growth.   I.e., count 3-5 whorls above breast height  (BH age)  and measure the corresponding height.

Generally, GI models give more accurate estimates of site index  than the conventional  Site Index models for two reasons:

  1. GI models are formulated to predicted site index  directly.  Whereas, Site Index models predict height and must be inverted to predict site index , which introduces a small amount of bias;

  2.  GI models are fit for each age, whereas Site Index models are fit over all ages simultaneously.
