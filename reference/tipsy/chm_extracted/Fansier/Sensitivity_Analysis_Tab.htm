The Sensitivity Analysis Tab examines 3 economic results (SV, NPV, IRR) with respect to variations in 5 key economic inputs.

Given the uncertainty surrounding forecasts of future economic conditions, it is prudent to undertake a sensitivity analysis of a regime's inputs and results. Sensitivity analysis involves varying key input values by some amount, e.g., +/- 10%.  If a change has little impact, the results are relatively robust for that particular input and its underlying assumptions (e.g., yields, costs and prices).  However, if a significant change in economic results occurs, then the uncertainty surrounding that input's underlying assumptions should be given greater weight in the decision making process.

Select an Economic Result

Use the drop-down pick-list to select one of three (3) economic results to analyze:

Site Value (SV); Net Present Value (NPV); or Internal Rate of Return (IRR)

Analysis Table

The analysis table contains the following columns:

  • Years Since Disturbance (yr) - even when using a height based regime, the age will be displayed here as it is needed for economic calculations.

  • Top Ht. (m) - The average height of the 100 trees/ha of largest DBH.

  • Base ($/ha) - The base economic result using unaltered inputs.

  • Discount +1% ($/ha) - what if discount rate increases by 1%?

  • Discount -1% ($/ha) - what if discount rate decreases by1%?

  • Regen. +10% ($/ha) - what if regeneration1 costs are 10% higher than expected?

  • Regen. -10% ($/ha) - what if regeneration1 costs are 10% lower than expected?

  • Post Regen. +10% ($/ha) - what if post-regeneration2 silviculture treatment costs are 10% higher than expected?

  • Post Regen. -10% ($/ha) - post-regeneration2silviculture treatment costs are 10% lower than estimated?

  • Conv. +10% ($/ha) - what if conversion3 costs are 10% higher than expected?

  • Conv. -10% ($/ha) - what if conversion3 costs are 10% lower than expected?

  • Revenue +10% ($/ha) - what if harvest revenue is 10% more than expected?

  • Revenue -10% ($/ha) - what if harvest revenue is 10% less than expected?

1 Regeneration costs include; surveys, site prep., tree improvement, planting, and brushing.l.

2 Post-regeneration silviculture treatment costs include; pre-commercial thinning, fertilization, pruning, etc., plus other silviculture costs.

3 Conversion Costs include; Harvesting, Commercial Thin Additives, Roads, Annual, and Manufacturing costs,.
