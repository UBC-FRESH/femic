Incremental IRR (Reinvestment method, FFT alternative)

Internal Rate of Return (IRR) is one of several methods for evaluating return on investment (ROI).  Simply put, IRR represents the interest rate returned on an investment.  It is also the discount rate that produces a NPV equal to zero.  FAN$IER calculates and reports three forms of Internal Rate of Return (IRR):

IRR (%)

IRR refers to simple IRR calculated independently for each regime.  It is reported in the Results, Report, and Compare Tabs.  It cannot be calculated for regimes with no treatment costs, e.g., base case (untreated) regimes when sunk costs are ignored.

Incremental IRR (%)

Incremental IRR is IRR calculated from the differences in costs and benefits between two mutually-exclusive investment options, ideally of equal length.   FAN$IER can display two forms of incremental-IRR in the Compare Tab,  Both are toggled on and off independently within the EDIT menu.   Both support return on investment (ROI) calculations specific to the Ministry’s Forests for Tomorrow Program (FFT).

FFT users are directed to FFT’s online documentation and training materials for a detailed explanation and instructions for FFT business use.

FFT Incremental IRR (%)

FFT Incremental IRR (%) is reported when comparing two regimes only if the age at base year for the base-case is greater than zero and no treatment costs were incurred afterward (i.e., the typical FFT base-case regime ignoring sunk costs).  The calculation method closely approximates the one used in FFT’s previous economic analysis software, the IRR-Workbook (an MS-Excel application).  Consistent with the IRR-Workbook method, the treated regime Site Value the discounted net value of the summation of discounted benefits and costs which would result from an infinite series of rotations of the same given length. Also known as soil rent, bare land value and soil expectation value. (SV) is added to the revenue stream of both regimes, before cost/revenue subtractions, such that the SVs cancel each other out when rotation lengths are equal.

FFT incremental IRR (2nd root) (%) this second value is only reported when the rotation length of the treated-case is shorter than that of the base-case.   Mathematically, this scenario always produces two IRR solutions (roots).  Due to MS-Excel limitations the IRR-Workbook only reported one of them. There is no consensus among economists as to which, if either, root is correct.  By reporting the second root, FAN$IER alerts the user to this issue.

Incremental IRR (Reinvestment method, FFT alternative)

To address IRR problems associated with different rotation lengths, FAN$IER developers introduced this alternative method for computing incremental-IRR.  Although the method has theoretical problems of its own, it provides an alternate comparison when FFT Incremental IRR is suspect.  In this method, net harvest revenues from the shorter of the two rotations are “reinvested” (discounted forward) at a fixed-interest rate (2% default) to the longer regime’s harvest date, in effect, equalizing the two investment periods (rotation lengths).

NOTE: When rotation lengths are equal, the two incremental-IRR methods produce the same (correct) answer.  In all other situations, incremental-IRR warrants a cautious, conservative interpretation.

A number of values are reported when the Reinvestment method is selected.:

  • Incremental IRR (%) - The IRR calculated using the reinvestment method.

The remaining rows in the section document the calculation:

  • Discount Period (yrs) - The larger of the two discount periods is applied to both stands.

  • Discount Rate (%) - The discount rate used in this calculation is equal to the calculated reinvestment IRR.

  • Reinvestment Interest Rate (%) - This is the opportunity cost of capital.  In this case, it is the assumed interest rate returned on the reinvested net benefit received from the shorter rotation.  The default is 2% (the FFT IRR threshold); but it can be changed in the Discount Assumptions definition.

  • Final Harvest Reinvestment Period (yrs) - The number of years the net harvest benefits from the shorter rotation are being reinvested.

  • Non-discounted Interest ($/ha) - The total interest before being discounted to 2006 dollars.

  • Discounted Interest ($/ha) - The total interest after being discounted to 2006 dollars.

  • Discounted Harvest Benefits ($/ha) - The total harvest benefits after being discounted to 2006 dollars.

  • Total Discounted Benefits ($/ha) - Sum of  discounted interest and discounted benefits.

  • Discounted Treatment Costs ($/ha) - Sum of treatment costs in 2006 dollars.

  • Discounted Harvesting Costs ($/ha) - Sum of harvesting costs in 2006 dollars.

  • Total Discounted Costs ($/ha) - Sum of all costs in 2006 dollars.

  • NPV ($/ha) - Total Discounted Benefits minus Total Discounted Costs (B-C).
