Forecasting Future Economic Conditions

An analyst must make either explicit or implicit forecasts of future market conditions when examining the economic efficiency of silviculture investments. Such forecasts are fraught with uncertainty as the return from these silviculture investments won't be obtained for 40 to 120 years. (The advantage of such long planning horizons is that the analyst will be dead long before anyone can prove him wrong.) Nevertheless, such forecasts must be made and an explicit statement of future price levels and costs allows for a critical review of the results.

We can remove the uncertainty of future rates of inflation from the analysis by measuring all revenues and costs associated with a treatment in constant dollar terms. FAN$IER measures all prices and costs in constant 2006 dollars. Values, when measured in constant dollars, are known as “real prices and costs” in the parlance of economics.

We also measure discount rates in real terms, that is we deduct the effects of current inflation on current discount rates. An example will clarify this point. If the current discount rate is 6% and the current inflation rate is 2% then the real discount rate is 4%. Note that real discount rates can be used only when all prices and costs are also measured in real terms.

We can use past price trends to help forecast future prices. Market Analysis examines trends in real prices for lumber and wood chips, and the likelihood of future real price increases.

There is also a similar review for coastal log prices. The review provides default real prices for each species. With FAN$IER, you can include future real price and cost increases in your analysis. You should, however, be prepared to justify any price and cost increases you choose.

For TIPSY users, keep in mind that TIPSY contains some species for which there is no database generated by TASS. These species use site index equations that were developed for that particular species, but TIPSY substitutes a database and many other default values. For example, western larch uses most default values for interior Douglas-fir. As a result of this 'blanket' substitution, some defaults are not based on the best available information. Replace the defaults with localized information whenever possible. The substitutions are displayed at the top of each printed table, or in the Stand Description.
