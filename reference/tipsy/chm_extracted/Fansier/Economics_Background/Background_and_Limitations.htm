Background

In British Columbia, both government and the forest industry spend hundreds of millions of dollars every year on silvicultural treatments to ensure a sustainable flow of timber into the future. Table 1-1 below lists estimates of the expenditures from 1989/90 to 2006/07. FAN$IER is an economic evaluation module designed to give B.C. forest managers a convenient tool to undertake stand level economic analysis of silviculture treatments on Crown (public) or private land. It can perform economic analysis on the silviculture treatments simulated by growth and yield models that can produce product-based yield output.

FAN$IER is not a stumpage calculation model. The Province's stumpage appraisal system attempts to determine a "fair" return to the Crown for timber harvested from Crown land. In doing this, the stumpage system uses current market prices and costs to determine the current worth of a stand. FAN$IER on the other hand, attempts to estimate the total possible return to all of society from a silvicultural investment, and not how the resource rent is divided among subsets of society. In doing this, the model is not concerned with current prices and costs; rather we need estimates of future prices and costs in order to determine the future return from silvicultural investments made today. This is not to say that we are not interested in recent prices and costs; rather we need to remove the cyclical nature of current price fluctuations in order to provide an estimate of probable future price and cost levels.

Limitations

Limitations affecting FAN$IER analyses include the assumptions inherent in the growth and yield input and the economic assumptions made within FAN$IER itself.  The latter includes cost and price defaults and user-supplied inputs and default over-rides.

Log and Lumber Yields

The bucking simulator (SmartAxe) used to derive TASS and TIPSY log recovery tables attempts to maximize value based on the same default log prices found in FAN$IER. Radical changes in log prices could change the volume cut into different grades.

Similarly, lumber and mill residue (e.g., wood chip) yields are based on sawmill simulations (SAWSIM) that attempt to maximize value recovery for dimension lumber (specifically, 2x4, 2x6, 2x8 and 2x10 inch boards).  Lumber prices drive value maximization by defining lumber dimension priorities.  Lumber prices used in sawmill simulations match the defaults in FAN$IER.  Lumber and Mill Residue Prices reviews past trends in lumber prices by dimension, and finds the price ratios for the various dimensions to be quite stable. Nevertheless, the inability to change the cutting patterns which determine lumber yields by dimension is a limitation of the database that may affect the results.

Default Costs

FAN$IER contains default cost values for a wide range of silviculture and harvesting activities, some are embedded in equations used to calculate things like harvesting costs (below), etc.  These same defaults were used in the TIPSY Economist and FAN$Y.  They were first derived in the mid 1990’s using Revenue Branch data, then adjusted in 2008 with 2006 data.  FAN$IER allows users to override each of these defaults.  

Caution:  Uninformed selective overriding of defaults is generally discouraged, since it can disturb the long-term historic relationships among costs and prices that are reflected in the defaults.

Harvesting Costs

Hauling includes log transport from the landing to the point of appraisal (log dump). For interior districts, hauling costs depend on the weighted average cycle time. A cycle includes loading, hauling, weighing, unloading, return time and unavoidable delay or idle time. Coastal districts use one-way road hauling costs plus additional towing or barging charges.

All costs (CT, if applicable, plus final harvest) are accumulated to the selected harvest age. and tree-to-truck harvest costs.

While FAN$IER allows changes the coefficients for some variables in the harvest cost equations, it does not allow the users to add variables.

Localizing Costs

FAN$IER provides district or region specific defaults for most silviculture, road development and harvest costs. The road and overhead costs are based on harvest conditions in each district. Even if District boundaries change, these defaults still provide a starting point analysis.  Users may substitute better site specific data if available. E.g., coastal second-growth stands where much of the necessary road development work may already be in place.  Although, be sure to review all costs to ensure appropriate relationships are maintained between associated costs.
